# Tree style analysis

The tree library belongs to the same visual universe as the Faces collection: near-black fineliner marks, warm fibrous paper, restrained dusty color, conspicuous asymmetry, and economy of detail. Structural character comes before botanical precision.

Trunks are irregular closed paths rather than rectangles. Recursive limbs taper, bend abruptly, terminate unevenly, cross occasionally, and split with unequal lengths. Foliage is visual shorthand—rough overlapping masses, curls, hooks, dots, broken contours, hatching, and rare black patches—with open paper preserved between clusters.

At contact-sheet scale, crown width, trunk height, lean, gaps, and branch spread remain the primary identifiers. At larger scales, bark strokes, terminal branch wobble, roots, fruit dots, and leaf marks become visible. Muted moss, clay, mist-blue, and lilac washes appear selectively and remain subordinate to ink.

Nepali grounding is ecological and contextual rather than taxonomic: hill broadleaf forms, wind-shaped mountain trees, orchard forms, sal/peepal-inspired shade silhouettes, roadside pruning, bamboo, sparse conifers, and lowland palms coexist without claiming scientific species accuracy.
