# Nepal roads + streetscape typology

A street resolves as context → function → topography → surface → width → asymmetric edges → drainage → pedestrian space → utilities → building relationship → vegetation/furniture/activity → maintenance → hand-drawn variation.

## Context families

Dense urban, urban residential, historic urban, semi-urban / peri-urban, small-town bazaar, rural settlement, rural agricultural, hill/mountain road, Terai/lowland road and forest road.

## Cross-section logic

Every street stores ordered left and right edge systems. Typical sections include shop–threshold–drain–road–drain–shop; building–sidewalk–drain–road–shoulder–wall; and field–ditch–gravel road–ditch–terrace wall. Opposite edges are normally different.

## Compatibility

- Historic urban favors narrow shared masonry/paved lanes and disfavors highway geometry.
- Mountain roads favor curves, retaining structures, catch drainage and asymmetric cut/valley edges.
- Peri-urban corridors favor mixed edges, construction, workshops and incomplete pedestrian infrastructure.
- Terai bazaars favor flat geometry, shaded commercial frontage and wider shoulders.

## Representation rules

Road geometry, topography, drainage and edges must remain legible without buildings or labels. Infrastructure is visible but not sensationalized. Damage, litter, animals, temples, wire density and congestion are optional conditions—not shorthand for Nepal.
